# Writing Style Guide for Thesis

## Purpose
This file defines the writing style that **must** be followed for any future edits of the LaTeX source files (chapters, sections, etc.).  It can be referenced by any AI assistant, external editor, or manual rewrite process to guarantee consistent, human‑like academic prose.

## General Principles
- **Academic register** – keep the tone formal, measured, and direct. No slang, jokes, or casual contractions.
- **Preserve meaning** – never change claims, numbers, data, or conclusions.
- **Keep citations untouched** – the exact `\cite{…}` command and its key list must remain unchanged.
- **Leave LaTeX structure intact** – do not edit `\label`, `\ref`, `\eqref`, `\textcite`, math environments, figure/table environments, custom macros, or comments.
- **Maintain terminology** – use the terms, abbreviations, and notation listed in the glossary (see `REWRITE_PLAN.md`).

## Anti‑AI Writing Rules (what to avoid)
| Category | Examples to avoid | Preferred alternative |
|----------|-------------------|-----------------------|
| **Filler vocabulary** | `delve`, `tapestry`, `realm`, `pivotal`, `robust`, `leverage`, `foster`, `showcase`, `underscore`, `shed light on`, `pave the way`, `navigate`, `unlock`, `cutting‑edge`, `ever‑evolving`, `in today's world`, `it is important to note that`, `in conclusion` | Use concrete wording: e.g., `examine`, `explain`, `demonstrate`, `highlight` (as a verb only when appropriate), `show`
| **Repetitive structures** | Repeating “not only X but also Y”, “it is not X, but rather Y”, three‑item lists, identical paragraph shape (topic sentence + three supports + wrap‑up) | Vary sentence length, combine ideas, omit unnecessary wrap‑up sentences
| **Over‑use of transitions** | Starting many sentences with `Moreover`, `Furthermore`, `Additionally` | Begin some sentences directly with the subject or use a different transition (`Thus`, `Consequently`, `As a result`)
| **Em dash for drama** | `—` used for emphasis | Use commas, periods, or parentheses instead
| **‑ing verb endings** | `...highlighting the importance of...` | Use simple verb forms: `highlights the importance of …`
| **Empty intensifiers** | `significantly`, `substantially`, `remarkably` without supporting data | Remove or replace with quantitative description if available

## Positive Style Recommendations
- **Vary sentence length** – mix short, medium, and long sentences.
- **Concrete verbs** – prefer `use` over `utilize`, `show` over `showcase`.
- **Subject‑first sentences** – allow sentences to start directly with the subject, no transition needed.
- **Cut padding** – if a sentence adds no new claim or citation, shorten or delete it.
